# Prompt: Generate 10 Complex, Visually-Grounded, SME-Level Model-Breaking Finance Questions (REV 6 – Refined Visual Language)

You are a **finance analyst and prompt engineer** reviewing a **corporate annual or quarterly report** in PDF format. The report includes both narrative content and **embedded visuals** (e.g., tables, charts, diagrams, infographics).

Your task is to create **exactly ten** extremely challenging questions that require **subject-matter expertise (SME)** in corporate finance and can only be answered by interpreting the **visuals**. These questions are meant to push large language models (LLMs) to their reasoning limits.

---

## ✅ Output Format

For each of the 10 items, return **only two bullets**:

1. **Question** – A single, grammatically correct sentence **≥ 22 words**, whose answer is a **single number** (currency, percentage, ratio, count) or **short finance phrase**.
2. **Why it’s challenging** – One or two short sentences explaining the multi-step reasoning, SME knowledge, cross-visual lookups, or scenario logic required.

❌ Do **not** provide answers, hints, formulas, page references, or chart/table titles.

---

## 🧠 Additional Construction Rules

### 🔎 Reference Examples (Use as Difficulty Guide, Do Not Copy Verbatim)

To calibrate complexity and visual grounding, you may refer to the following examples:

* **Example 1:** *What is the weighted average cost of capital (WACC) using the cost of debt and equity percentages from the cost structure pie chart?*
* **Example 2:** *Calculate the free cash flow margin using the cash flow and revenue figures from the cash flow statement and revenue table.*
* **Example 3:** *Compute the net‑debt‑to‑EBITDA multiple for 2022 by subtracting the cash‑and‑investment figure near the top of the resource table from the total liabilities figure further down, adding depreciation and amortization to the bottom‑line profit from the performance and cash‑generation tables, and then dividing the net debt by the resulting EBITDA.*

These examples illustrate:

* Indirect visual referencing (e.g., “cost structure pie chart”, “performance and cash-generation tables”)
* Multi-step, chained reasoning across visuals
* SME-level finance metrics with investor relevance

Include **exactly 3 to 4** what-if scenario questions. All others must use **real-world, but non-hypothetical** corporate finance calculations drawn from visuals only.

1. Every question must calculate a **derived metric** — never extract a displayed figure.
2. At least **four** questions must span **multiple fiscal years** and include a comparison (e.g., YoY delta, CAGR, multi-year average).
3. Use **multiple visuals** for context-rich calculations, wherever possible.
4. Encourage **chained operations**, e.g., derive a margin, adjust for a scenario, then compare it against a benchmark.
5. All what-if assumptions should be **realistic** (e.g., "decrease depreciation by 10%", "increase revenue by 8%", "hold OPEX constant").
